Your gut does more than digest

Your gut is connected to your brain, your immune system and your body's natural filters. Here's how they work together.

Your "second brain"

Ever had a gut feeling? There's a reason for it. Your gut has its own nervous system, called the enteric nervous system, lining your digestive tract from top to bottom.

More than 100 million nerve cells, and by some estimates closer to 500 million

It talks back and forth with your brain all day long. That's why we care for your gut as part of caring for your whole self.

Sources: Johns Hopkins Medicine, Johns Hopkins Biomedical Odyssey

Your gut and your lymphatic system

Tiny lymph vessels in your small intestine, called lacteals, absorb fats and fat-soluble vitamins from your food. Your gut lining also holds a large share of your immune tissue.

About 600 lymph nodes, and no heart to pump lymph

Lymph moves with your muscles and your breathing. That's why movement, deep breathing and whole-body vibration are part of how we care for the whole body.

Sources: Cleveland Clinic, National Cancer Institute SEER, Mucosal Immunology

Your liver and kidneys

Your body has its own cleansing team. All the blood leaving your stomach and intestines passes through your liver, which makes bile to help carry away waste and digest fats.

Your kidneys filter about 150 quarts of blood a day

Our approach is simple: support the whole team with water, nourishing food, movement and rest.

Sources: Johns Hopkins Medicine, NIH (NIDDK)

A little history

The 3,000-year-old wellness practice making a comeback

Most wellness trends come and go in a season. This one has lasted more than 3,000 years.

Fun fact: the Battle Creek Sanitarium, where colon hydrotherapy was a centerpiece, was run by Dr. John Harvey Kellogg, the man better known for Corn Flakes.
  1. More than 3,500 years ago

    Ancient Egypt

    Water cleansing shows up in Egyptian medical writings, including the Ebers Papyrus.

  2. The ancient world

    Greece, Rome and China

    Physicians across the ancient world practiced water cleansing.

  3. Early 1900s

    Battle Creek, Michigan

    Colon hydrotherapy was a centerpiece of famous American health retreats.

  4. Today

    Boise, Idaho

    It's making a comeback with people who are serious about natural wellness. They read labels, choose whole foods, and want something hands-on that works with the body instead of one more bottle in the cabinet.

Sources: Gastrointestinal Society, Best Practice Journal (NZ)
